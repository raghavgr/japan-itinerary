Arashiyama at Dawn · Bamboo Grove · Kinkaku-ji · Ryoan-ji · Tenzan Onsen

Friday, March 27
▼
🏨 R Star Hostel Kyoto · Kawaramachi area · 15min by JR to Arashiyama · 25min bus to Kinkaku-ji
  • 06:30 — Depart for Arashiyama
    JR Sagano Line Kyoto → Saga-Arashiyama 15min ¥240 ($1.63) Arrive before 7AM — the bamboo grove is empty at this hour. The light through the bamboo canopy at dawn is transformative.
  • 07:00 — Sagano Bamboo Grove
    Free. Walk the main path before the tourist wave arrives (it gets very crowded from 9:30AM). The bamboo rustles and creaks — a sound UNESCO designated as one of Japan's 100 soundscapes to preserve. Morning mist on cool March days.
    📸 Bamboo corridor with morning light shafts · Near-empty path looking up at canopy · Motion blur of passing bicycle
  • 07:45 — Togetsu-kyo Bridge
    The iconic arched bridge over Oi River with Arashiyama mountain backdrop. Walk across and back. Cherry trees along both riverbanks bloom late March.
    📸 Bridge reflection in still river at dawn · Mountain + sakura + bridge three-layer composition
  • 08:30 — Tenryu-ji Temple Garden
    ¥500 ($3.40) garden only, ¥800 ($5.44) inc. main hall. UNESCO. Pond garden with Arashiyama mountain as borrowed scenery. One of Kyoto's oldest and most serene gardens. Nearly empty this early.
  • 10:00 — Bus to Kinkaku-ji area
    Bus 11 or 59 Arashiyama → Kinkakuji-michi ~35min ¥230 ($1.56)
  • 10:30 — Kinkaku-ji (金閣寺) — Golden Pavilion
    ¥500 ($3.40). The three-storey gilded pavilion reflected in Kyoko-chi pond. Arrive before 11AM — tour buses dominate from 11:30. The path is one-way and takes 20min. Every angle of this temple is composed for photography.
    📸 Golden pavilion + perfect pond reflection · Top-floor gold detail close-up · Pine tree framing pavilion
  • 11:30 — Ryoan-ji (龍安寺) — Rock Garden
    ¥600 ($4.10). 15min walk from Kinkaku-ji. The famous karesansui dry garden — 15 stones arranged so that from any viewpoint, only 14 are visible. Sit on the wooden veranda and let it work on you. The stone garden is thought to be ~500 years old.
  • 12:30 — Lunch near Ryoan-ji
    Tofu kaiseki is the local specialty — try Yudofu Sagano or nearby tofu restaurant. ¥1,500–3,000 ($10–20).
  • 14:00 — Return Arashiyama: Tenzan Onsen (天山の湯)
    Bus back to Arashiyama ~30min Hidden local onsen, 5min walk from Arashiyama station. Outdoor rotenburo with natural stone tubs. ¥1,000 ($6.80). NOT a tourist trap — this is where locals go. Multiple baths including outdoor, hot, and cold plunge.
    No tattoo ban. Bring a small towel or rent one for ¥200. The outdoor bath in late March has occasional cherry petals falling in.
  • 16:30 — Return Kyoto
    JR Sagano Saga-Arashiyama → Kyoto 15min
  • 19:30 — Pontocho Dinner
    Pontocho alley — narrow lantern-lit corridor parallel to Kamo River. Best Kyoto dining street. Budget ¥3,000–5,000 ($20–34) for a sit-down meal. In March, the riverside yuka platforms (riverside terraces) start opening.

Fushimi Inari at Sunrise · Keage Incline · Philosopher's Path · Kifune Evening Visit

Saturday, March 28
▼
🏨 R Star Hostel Kyoto → checking out tonight · 5min by JR to Fushimi Inari · 15min metro to Keage · Return from Kifune ~21:00
  • 05:30 — Depart for Fushimi Inari
    JR Nara Line Kyoto → Inari 5min ¥150 ($1.02) Arrive at the gate by 05:45–06:00. The thousands of vermilion torii tunnels winding up the mountain are completely empty at this hour. Misty, silent, one of the most profound experiences in Kyoto.
    Fushimi Inari is one of the most photographed places in Japan. From 8AM it becomes a wall-to-wall crowd. The mountain above (Yotsutsuji viewpoint, ~45min hike) stays emptier all day. If you don't hike early, it won't be the same.
  • 06:00 — Fushimi Inari Taisha (伏見稲荷大社)
    Free, open 24hrs. Walk through the main lower torii gates (10min), then hike up through the tunnel corridors. The Senbon Torii (thousand torii) section is the classic view. Climb to Yotsutsuji (half-mountain viewpoint, 30–40min hike) for dawn views over Kyoto. Return down by 08:00.
    📸 Looking up the torii tunnel at first light · Pre-dawn mist in the gate corridors · City panorama from Yotsutsuji at sunrise
  • 08:15 — Breakfast near Inari station
    Street stall inari-zushi (fox sushi — rice in fried tofu skin, the food offering of Inari shrine) from vendors near the shrine entrance. ¥200–400.
  • 09:30 — Keage Incline (蹴上インクライン)
    Kyoto Metro Tozai Line Fushimi → Keage 2 stops ¥220 ($1.50) The disused railway incline is lined with ~90 cherry trees that bloom in late March — one of the most photogenic and least-known sakura spots in Kyoto. The rusty old rail tracks + pink blossoms is a classic composition. Only a 10-minute walk end to end, but worth 30min of photos.
    📸 Looking down the incline tracks through the sakura tunnel · Old rail cart with blossoms · Close-up of tracks between cherry tree roots
  • 11:00 — Nanzen-ji Temple
    5-min walk from Keage. Free grounds. The massive Sanmon gate ¥600 ($4.10) to climb. The surreal Roman-style red-brick aqueduct running through the temple grounds — one of the most unexpected and photogenic structures in Kyoto. Temple garden ¥500 ($3.40).
    📸 Brick aqueduct with moss and greenery · Looking through the aqueduct arches
  • 12:30 — Philosopher's Path (哲学の道)
    reel Start at the Nanzen-ji end, walk north 1.5km along the canal lined with 500 cherry trees. The most famous sakura walk in Japan. 30–45min with stops. Cafes and galleries along the way.
    Walk before noon or after 4PM. This is peak sakura week — midday is manageable but early/late is better.
    📸 Canal + cherry blossom reflections in water · Pink petal canopy overhead · Cafe interior through shoji screen
  • 14:00 — Heian Shrine Garden
    Massive orange torii gate (largest in Japan), weeping cherry trees inside the garden. ¥600 ($4.10) garden entrance. One of the most composed sakura views in Kyoto.
  • 15:30 — Ippodo Tea (一保堂茶舗)
    Founded 1717. Teramachi-Nijo, 10-min walk. THE place to buy matcha to take home.
    MATCHA TO TAKE HOME: Ippodo Ichinomai ceremonial grade ¥2,200 ($15, 40g tin). Tsujiri Shijo flagship for everyday drinking. Marukyu Koyamaen for professional grade. The in-store tea bar: ¥1,200 ($8.15) to taste before buying. Load up here — you're going to Wazuka tomorrow for the source.
  • 17:30 — Evening trip to Kifune 🏮
    Kyoto Metro Karasuma Line → Kokusaikaikan (northern terminus) 25min ¥280 ($1.90) Eizan Railway Kokusaikaikan → Kibuneguchi 28min ¥420 ($2.86) Total ~55min from central Kyoto. Kifune is a narrow mountain valley north of the city — completely different atmosphere from the city.
  • 18:30 — Kifune Shrine at dusk 🏮
    The stone lantern-lined path leading to Kifune Shrine lit at dusk is one of Kyoto's most magical sights, especially in late March when the surrounding forest is still transitioning to spring. Free. The shrine is dedicated to the god of water — the Kamo River originates near here.
    📸 Stone lantern pathway lit orange at dusk · Shrine torii with cedar trees at night · Lantern reflections in the stream
  • 19:30 — Dinner in Kifune valley
    Several riverside restaurants serve kaiseki and small-course meals — you don't need to stay overnight to eat here. Hirobun and Kibune Fujiya take dinner reservations for non-guests. Budget ¥5,000–10,000 ($34–$68). OR head back to Kyoto for a cheaper option.
  • 21:00 — Return to R Star Hostel Kyoto
    Eizan Railway Kibuneguchi → Demachiyanagi 28min ¥420 ($2.86) Metro → hostel

  • 07:00 — Tokyo → Kanazawa
    Hokuriku Shinkansen Kagayaki Tokyo → Kanazawa 2h28min ¥14,320 ($97.41) — JR Pass ✓ Arrive Kanazawa ~09:30. This is the most underrated city in Japan — former castle town that escaped WWII bombing, preserved geisha districts, world-class garden, incredible seafood market.
    Kanazawa is often called "Little Kyoto" but it's more real than Kyoto — fewer tourists, locals actually live the traditional culture. The city is compact and easily walkable from the station.
  • 09:45 — Kenroku-en Garden (兼六園)
    City loop bus from station → Kenroku-en 20min ¥210 ($1.43) ¥320 ($2.18). One of Japan's three most celebrated traditional gardens. 11.7 hectares with six attributes (spaciousness, seclusion, artifice, antiquity, water, and views). In early April: cherry blossoms throughout the garden, new green, extraordinary.
    📸 Kotoji stone lantern + pond reflection — the iconic Kenroku-en image · Weeping cherry by the stream · Garden overview from the upper terrace
  • 11:00 — Kanazawa Castle Park
    Free. Adjacent to Kenroku-en. The castle is partially restored — the Ishikawa-mon gate is original and spectacular. Moat walk with sakura.
  • 12:00 — Omicho Market (近江町市場)
    City loop bus or 15min walk "Kanazawa's kitchen" — the freshest seafood market in Japan outside of Tsukiji. Kanazawa faces the Sea of Japan and gets different fish than Tokyo. Try: kaisen-don (seafood rice bowl) ¥2,000–4,000 ($13.60–$27.20) — crab, uni, salmon, ikura on rice. Several restaurants on the upper floor of the market. Queue by 11:45 for the best spots.
    Kanazawa specialty: nodoguro (blackthroat sea perch) — a fatty white fish prized across Japan. Absolutely worth ordering if on the menu.
  • 14:00 — Higashi Chaya District (東茶屋街)
    15min walk from Omicho. The best-preserved geisha district in Japan. Two-story ochaya teahouses with wooden lattice facades lining a quiet street. One teahouse (Shima, ¥750/$5.10) is open as a museum showing the interiors. Gold leaf crafts shops (Kanazawa produces 99% of Japan's gold leaf — buy gold leaf ice cream ¥750 for the photo).
    📸 Higashi Chaya wooden lattice row · Gold leaf ice cream · Shima teahouse interior tatami rooms
  • 15:30 — Nishi Chaya (optional) or 21st Century Museum
    21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art ¥1,100 ($7.48) — famous for the swimming pool installation (Leandro Erlich) where people stand inside a room under water while others walk on top. Free zones open, paid zones include the pool. 15min walk from Higashi Chaya.

    Hokuriku Shinkansen Kagayaki Kanazawa → Tokyo 2h28min — JR Pass ✓ Arrive Tokyo ~19:30. Dinner near hotel.
